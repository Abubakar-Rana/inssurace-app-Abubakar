 A.  INTAKE

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 M1  MAIL INGESTION                                                      LIVE
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

 PURPOSE
   Bring inbound certificate requests into the system from the agency's
   mailbox.

 INPUT     A designated mailbox, read over IMAP.
 OUTPUT    Request records, one per genuine certificate request.

 BEHAVIOUR
   Connects to the mailbox and reads recent messages. Each message is passed
   to classification (M2) BEFORE anything is written to the database.

 DESIGN DECISIONS AND WHY
   - The mailbox is opened READ-ONLY. The server itself refuses any attempt to
     change a flag, so the system cannot mark, move or delete the agency's
     mail under any circumstance, including a bug.

   - De-duplication is a unique database constraint on the message ID, NOT the
     "seen" flag. This is what makes repeated polling safe: two pollers running
     at once, or a crash mid-run, cannot produce duplicate requests.

   - Concurrent callers are coalesced. Several open dashboards share a single
     mailbox read, with a minimum interval enforced per agency, so the number
     of open browser tabs does not multiply the load on Gmail.

 CURRENT LIMITATION
   Authenticates with a Google App Password. This is suitable for a mailbox
   the agency owns and operates, but it grants full account access and cannot
   be scoped or revoked per integration. Production requires OAuth 2.0 scoped
   to read-only mail. The replacement is confined to a single module.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 M2  REQUEST CLASSIFICATION                                              LIVE
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

 PURPOSE
   Decide whether an inbound message is a certificate request at all.

 INPUT     Sender, subject and body of one message.
 OUTPUT    A verdict, a confidence score, and a human-readable reason.

 WHY THIS MODULE EXISTS
   A shared agency mailbox is mostly not certificate requests. It carries
   renewal notices, loss runs, newsletters, calendar invites and spam.
   Ingesting all of it would put the entire mailbox on the reviewer's screen
   and, worse, store the contents of unrelated correspondence in a system
   built to hold certificate requests.

 DESIGN DECISIONS AND WHY
   - Filtering happens BEFORE the database write. Mail that is not a request
     is never stored at all. The cheapest possible answer to "what did you do
     with my email?" is "we never kept it."

   - Precision is favoured over recall, but every miss is visible. Each
     skipped subject and the reason for skipping it is returned to the
     dashboard and printed by the command-line tool. A false negative loses a
     customer's request, so it must never be silent.

   - Insurance vocabulary alone is not sufficient. A renewal notice says
     "policy" and "coverage" throughout without asking for anything. The
     threshold requires the document to be named: certificate of insurance,
     COI, or ACORD.

   - Senders that cannot receive a reply (no-reply addresses, mailer daemons)
     are refused on principle rather than as spam filtering. The system answers
     by replying in-thread; an address that discards replies cannot be the
     origin of a request it can fulfil.

 OVERRIDE
   A configuration flag disables filtering entirely for diagnosis, so an
   operator investigating a missing request can see whether it reached the
   mailbox at all.

 ASSURANCE   16 automated checks, 9 of which assert refusal.

 M3  INSURED EXTRACTION                                                  LIVE
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

 PURPOSE
   Read candidate insured company names out of the request text.

 INPUT     Subject and body.
 OUTPUT    Zero or more candidate names, ranked by how explicitly the email
           stated them, each with the phrase it came from.

 THE TRAP THIS MODULE EXISTS TO AVOID
   The company that SENDS a certificate request is usually not the company the
   certificate is FOR. A freight broker emails asking for a certificate on
   their carrier. The broker is the certificate holder; the carrier is the
   insured. The broker's name appears in the from-address, the signature and
   the footer, and it is the wrong answer every time.

 DESIGN DECISIONS AND WHY
   - Names are only accepted from positions that state who the certificate is
     FOR: after "COI for", behind an "Insured:" label, and similar. The
     sender's identity is never used as a source.

   - The body is truncated at the signature block before reading. The
     signature is the single richest source of wrong answers.

   - Extracting nothing is a VALID OUTCOME, not a failure. It means the email
     never said. That routes to a human, which is correct.

 ASSURANCE   17 automated checks. Runs without a database or network.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 M4  ENTITY RESOLUTION                                                   LIVE
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

 PURPOSE
   Turn a candidate name into a specific client record, or decline to.

 INPUT     A candidate name.
 OUTPUT    A matched client, or an explicit abstention with the candidates
           that were considered.

 BEHAVIOUR
   Ranks the client list by trigram similarity across legal names and known
   aliases, after suffix-aware normalisation ("Inc", "L.L.C.", "Company").
   A single indexed query; no machine learning, no external service.

 DESIGN DECISIONS AND WHY
   - A match requires BOTH a strong similarity score AND a clear margin over
     the runner-up. Two related entities never resolve on a narrow lead.
     "Smith Trucking LLC" and "Smith Trucking of Ohio LLC" are different
     companies with different policies.

   - Abstention routes to a human. It is not an error state.

   - Raising either threshold makes the system ask a human more often, which
     is the safe direction. This is a deliberate tuning property.

 A DECISION THAT WAS TRIED AND REVERSED
   An early version added a bonus when one name contained the other. Because
   "Smart Way Solutions" is contained in "Smart Way Solutions of Ohio", the
   bonus scored a DIFFERENT company at a perfect 1.00. The extra words in a
   longer name are exactly what distinguish two related entities. The bonus
   was removed and a permanent note added to prevent its return.

 ASSURANCE   24 automated checks, roughly half asserting refusal.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 M5  CERTIFICATE HOLDER RESOLUTION                                       LIVE
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

 PURPOSE
   Determine who the certificate should be addressed to.

 INPUT     The request email.
 OUTPUT    A holder name and address block, stored and reusable.

 WHY THIS IS THE ONLY FIELD READ FROM THE EMAIL
   Every other value on the certificate describes the insured, and the
   insured's own records are the only trustworthy source. The holder is
   different: the holder is whoever asked, and the agency has no record of
   them until they write in. Reading it from a stored list means printing
   whichever holder happened to be on file, which is how a certificate ends up
   addressed to a company that never requested it.

 DESIGN DECISIONS AND WHY
   - It reads only BELOW the sign-off. Insured extraction (M3) reads only
     ABOVE it. The two are deliberate mirror images:

         above the sign-off  ->  who the certificate is FOR
         below the sign-off  ->  who is ASKING for it

     This split is the safety property. A name in a signature can become the
     holder, which is an addressee, but never the insured, whose coverage the
     document actually asserts. The worst a bad read can do is address the
     certificate imperfectly, in a field the reviewer is looking straight at.

   - The LAST sign-off wins, not the first. "Thanks for the quick turnaround"
     appears mid-body constantly; the block wanted is at the bottom.

   - Phone numbers, bare email addresses and URLs are discarded. The ACORD
     holder box is a postal address block with four lines; contact details
     would consume them.

   - It is never empty. With no usable signature it falls back to the sender's
     display name and address - still the requester.

   - Holders are de-duplicated on the full block, so a repeat broker reuses one
     record, while two different people at the same brokerage stay distinct.

 ASSURANCE   12 automated checks, including an explicit test that neither
             reader can see the other's region of the email.

 M6  CERTIFICATE ASSEMBLY                                                LIVE
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

 PURPOSE
   Turn database records into a complete certificate object.

 INPUT     Producer, insured, holder, policies, vehicles.
 OUTPUT    A single certificate object containing every printed value.

 BEHAVIOUR
   Orders coverage rows to match the ACORD 25 grid, populates the free-form
   coverage rows (Motor Truck Cargo, Physical Damage and similar), composes
   the description of operations, and decides how much of the vehicle
   schedule fits on page one.

 DESIGN DECISIONS AND WHY
   - The module is PURE: given the same records it always produces the same
     object. It performs no database access and no I/O. This is what makes the
     output testable field by field without a running system.

   - Page-one capacity is computed from WRAPPED lines, not logical ones, using
     real font metrics. A vehicle entry is never split across the page break.

   - Endorsement and operations wording comes from the database and is printed
     verbatim. The system does not compose legal language.

 ASSURANCE   17 automated checks comparing generated output field-by-field
             against a certificate the agency produced manually.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 M7  FORM RENDERING                                                      LIVE
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

 PURPOSE
   Place the certificate object onto the ACORD forms, both on screen and as a
   PDF.

 OUTPUT    An editable on-screen form, and a print-ready PDF of one or two
           pages.

 THE CENTRAL ARCHITECTURAL IDEA
   The ACORD form is never re-drawn by hand. A SINGLE COORDINATE MAP defines
   where every field sits, and both renderers read from it:

         COORDINATE MAP  (one definition, in points)
              |
              +---->  On-screen form  (inputs positioned over the artwork)
              |
              +---->  PDF generator    (text stamped onto the blank form)

   Because both consume the same map, what a reviewer approves on screen and
   what the holder receives as a PDF cannot drift apart. Changing a position
   updates both automatically; no component contains a hardcoded coordinate.

 DESIGN DECISIONS AND WHY
   - Coordinates were MEASURED from a real filled certificate, not estimated.

   - Field heights are enforced. A block that would overflow its box is
     clamped rather than printed over the form's rules.

   - PDF output is DETERMINISTIC. Document metadata is pinned, so re-rendering
     the same certificate always produces byte-identical output. Without this,
     the stored document hash would be meaningless after any reprint.

 SECOND PAGE
   When the vehicle schedule exceeds page one, an ACORD 101 Additional Remarks
   Schedule is produced automatically and carries the remainder.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 M8  CERTIFICATE LIFECYCLE                                               LIVE
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

 PURPOSE
   Govern the states a certificate may occupy and the transitions between
   them.

 STATES    draft  ->  issued  ->  (superseded by a later revision)

 RULES ENFORCED IN CODE, NOT BY CONVENTION

   - DRAFTING IS AUTOMATIC; ISSUING IS NOT. The certificate is assembled the
     moment a request resolves to an insured. The reviewer's job is to CHECK a
     document, not to ask for one. Nothing about this weakens control: a draft
     is still only a draft.

   - AN ISSUED CERTIFICATE IS NEVER MUTATED. Editing is rejected once the
     status leaves draft.

   - A CORRECTION IS A NEW REVISION OF THE SAME NUMBER. A holder who received
     COI-2026-0001 must see that number again. A fresh number reads as a
     second, unrelated certificate and leaves the first apparently still in
     force.

   - REPRINTS RE-RENDER THE STORED SNAPSHOT, never live policy data. Policies
     renew; a reprint has to show what was certified on the day.

   - A DRAFTING FAILURE NEVER FAILS THE INTAKE RUN. The request reaches the
     dashboard either way. Losing a whole run of incoming mail because one
     client's policy data is incomplete would be the worse trade.

   - THE CLIENT NEVER SUPPLIES CERTIFICATE IDENTIFIERS. Submitted certificate
     numbers and form editions are overwritten with the stored ones.

 ASSURANCE   17 automated checks against a live database with security
             policies active.

 M9  DELIVERY                                                            LIVE
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

 PURPOSE
   Send an issued certificate to the requester.

 OUTPUT    A threaded email reply with the PDF attached, and a delivery
           record.

 BEHAVIOUR
   Replies inside the ORIGINAL email thread. A certificate that arrives as a
   new, unrelated message forces the requester to match it up by hand, and in
   a shared compliance inbox it is often missed entirely.

 RULES ENFORCED IN CODE

   - ONLY ISSUED CERTIFICATES ARE SENT. A draft is work in progress; emailing
     one puts an unreviewed document in a holder's hands, and they will rely
     on it.

   - NOTHING IS SENT AUTOMATICALLY. Approval and sending are separate human
     actions, deliberately. An agency must be able to issue a certificate and
     decide later whether, when and to whom it goes.

   - THE BYTES ARE VERIFIED BEFORE SENDING. The PDF is re-rendered from the
     stored snapshot and its hash compared against the hash recorded at
     approval. A mismatch aborts the send.

   - THE SEND HAPPENS BEFORE THE DATABASE RECORDS IT. If mail transmission
     fails, nothing is written and the reviewer retries. The opposite order
     would let the system record a delivery that never happened - worse than a
     retry, because the agency would believe the holder had the document.

   - THE REVIEWER CONFIRMS THE RECIPIENT. The address is pre-filled from the
     request and remains editable, because a compliance inbox is often not the
     person who wrote in.

 RECIPIENT CONTROLS
   A configurable allowlist governs who may be emailed. Reserved test domains
   are refused unconditionally and cannot be enabled, which is what prevents a
   click on demonstration data from emailing a real company.

 ASSURANCE   16 automated checks on the guardrails alone, plus a live
             end-to-end test against a real mailbox.

 M10  REVIEW DASHBOARD                                                   LIVE
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

 PURPOSE
   Show a reviewer what needs their attention, and let them act on it.

 SCREENS
   - Request inbox: every inbound request, its matched insured, and the state
     of its certificate.
   - Certificate review: both ACORD pages, editable, with approve and send.
   - Certificates: everything ever issued, with delivery history.

 BEHAVIOUR
   The browser does not poll anything. Changes are PUSHED to it by the server
   as they happen, and the page never reloads. There is no "check for new
   email" button because there is nothing for it to do.

 WHAT THE HEADER SHOWS
   - HOW MANY REQUESTS NOBODY HAS OPENED YET. Recorded against the request
     rather than the browser, so it reads the same on every machine and
     survives signing out.
   - WHETHER THE LIVE FEED IS ACTUALLY CONNECTED. Without this, a broken
     connection looks exactly like a quiet mailbox.

 DESIGN DECISIONS AND WHY
   - A CHECK THAT FOUND NOTHING SAYS NOTHING. Only an arrival draws a banner.
     A dashboard that redraws every few seconds teaches the reviewer to stop
     reading it, and then it is worth nothing on the day it matters.

   - LOSING THE CONNECTION IS VISIBLE. It reconnects on its own, and says so
     meanwhile. Silently ceasing to update is the worst available outcome: the
     screen looks calm while requests pile up unseen.

   - MAIL THE FILTER PASSED OVER IS PULLED, NOT PUSHED. Announcing every
     newsletter the agency receives would drown the notice that matters, so
     skipped messages are kept and shown on request instead.

 MEASURED PERFORMANCE
   7-8 seconds from a message landing in the mailbox to appearing on screen,
   with no user interaction. The sender's own provider typically adds another
   5-7 seconds before the message is visible to us at all.

 ASSURANCE   45 automated checks executed in a real browser.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 M11  MANUAL RESOLUTION                                                  LIVE
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

 PURPOSE
   Let a person settle a request the system declined to decide.

 BEHAVIOUR
   Presents the evidence rather than a blank field: what the extractor read
   from the email, why it stopped, the email itself, and a searchable list of
   insureds.

 WHY IT IS BUILT THIS WAY
   Abstention is only useful if a person can then decide. A reviewer who can
   see "it read 'Smart Way' and found two companies that both start that way"
   makes a better decision than one handed an empty search box.

   Choosing the insured is recorded against the user's account. It is worth
   auditing precisely because it overrides the machine: if a wrong certificate
   is ever issued, this records who chose the company.

   Once the insured is identified, the certificate is drafted immediately -
   identifying the insured was the only missing judgement.

 M12  IDENTITY AND ACCESS CONTROL                                     PARTIAL
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

 PURPOSE
   Establish who is acting, and confine them to their own agency's data.

 BEHAVIOUR
   Sign-in through the agency's own identity provider using OpenID Connect.
   Signed, stateless session cookies. Role-based permissions (administrator,
   reviewer, read-only).

 DESIGN DECISIONS AND WHY
   - NO PASSWORDS EXIST IN THE SYSTEM. There is deliberately no password
     column in the database. Nothing to phish, leak or reset; the agency's
     identity provider enforces multi-factor authentication.

   - AUTHORITY IS RE-READ FROM THE DATABASE ON EVERY REQUEST. The cookie is a
     claim of identity, not of permission. Suspending a user or changing a
     role takes effect immediately, on their existing session.

   - USERS ARE NEVER AUTO-CREATED AT SIGN-IN. A record must already exist, or
     anyone with a Google account could land inside an agency's data.

   - TENANT SEPARATION IS ENFORCED BY THE DATABASE, not by application
     filtering. Row-level security policies compare every row against the
     session's agency. A forgotten filter therefore returns nothing rather
     than another agency's certificates.

   - Rotating a single secret signs every user out - the "revoke all sessions"
     lever.

 VERIFIED   Unauthenticated requests refused; forged cookies refused;
            cross-origin writes refused; a suspended account refused on its
            existing session.

 REMAINING  Identity provider registration is a client-side configuration step
            and is not yet performed for production. Until it is, the system
            uses a development sign-in path that refuses to operate in a
            production environment - so a missing configuration locks everyone
            out rather than letting anyone in.

 M13  AUDIT AND EVIDENCE                                              PARTIAL
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

 PURPOSE
   Produce a record of what happened that can be relied upon afterwards.

 BEHAVIOUR
   Every state change writes an entry recording the actor, the action, the
   subject, and the shape of what changed.

 DESIGN DECISIONS AND WHY
   - APPEND-ONLY BY DATABASE GRANT. Update and delete are revoked from the
     application's database role. Immutability is enforced by the database,
     not promised by the application.

   - ENTRIES ARE HASH-CHAINED. Each entry incorporates the hash of its
     predecessor, so altering any entry invalidates every entry after it.
     Tampering becomes DETECTABLE, not merely forbidden.

   - THE LOG STORES REFERENCES AND HASHES, NEVER CONTENT. Email bodies are
     deliberately excluded. Embedding them would make the retention promise
     impossible to keep and would turn the audit log into the very personal
     data store that data minimisation exists to prevent.

   - THE LOG DECLARES NO FOREIGN KEYS. Every other table cascades from the
     agency record. If the audit log did too, honouring a deletion request
     would erase the record of what was done.

   - Audit entries are written in the SAME TRANSACTION as the change they
     record. An action and its record commit together or not at all.

 REMAINING  There is no user interface over the log. The data is complete and
            queryable; it has no screen.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 M14  DATA RETENTION AND ENCRYPTION                                   PARTIAL
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

 PURPOSE
   Hold as little as possible, for as short as possible, and protect what must
   be held.

 RETENTION
   Every stored email body carries a purge date, set at ingestion, currently
   30 days. Messages that are not certificate requests are never stored at
   all. A manual purge command exists today.

 ENCRYPTION DESIGN
   Envelope encryption with a per-agency data key, which is itself encrypted
   by a master key held outside the database. The stored key material is
   useless without the master key. Deleting an agency's key renders every
   ciphertext they own permanently unreadable - satisfying a deletion request
   without touching the immutable audit log.

   Secret values that must be searched by exact match are protected with a
   deterministic blind index, so lookup is possible without storing the value
   in readable form.

 REMAINING
   - The encryption module is implemented and tested but is NOT yet applied to
     the columns it is designed to protect.
   - The purge job is manual. A scheduled job reading the stored purge dates
     is required.

 M16  MAILBOX WATCHER AND LIVE FEED                                      LIVE
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

 PURPOSE
   Put a new request on the reviewer's screen within seconds, without anyone
   asking for it and without the page reloading.

 BEHAVIOUR
   A watcher process holds ONE connection to the mailbox open. Two things run
   on that connection:

     THE FAST PATH   the mail server itself announces new mail. Usually
                     sub-second.
     THE GUARANTEE   the same open connection is asked every couple of seconds
                     whether anything newer exists.

   When something arrives it is ingested and the browser is told. The browser
   then re-fetches through the normal, authenticated route.

 DESIGN DECISIONS AND WHY

   - RECONNECTING EVERY FEW SECONDS WOULD BE ABSURD. Doing so would mean around
     seventeen thousand mailbox logins a day to be told, nearly every time, that
     nothing changed. Holding one connection open makes a two-second check
     essentially free, which is what makes it affordable at all.

   - THE REQUEST IS ANNOUNCED BEFORE ITS CERTIFICATE IS BUILT. Assembling the
     document takes several more database operations. Making the reviewer wait
     for that before the request even appears is the difference between a
     dashboard that feels live and one that feels slow. The certificate follows
     a moment later and announces itself again.

   - THE MESSAGE TO THE BROWSER CARRIES NO DATA - only "something changed, go
     and look". This connection therefore cannot become a way to read records
     the normal route would have refused.

   - A LOST MESSAGE IS SURVIVABLE. Nothing about correctness depends on it: the
     dashboard still refreshes on its own slow timer and whenever the reviewer
     returns to the tab. The failure mode is a slower update, never a wrong
     screen.

   - THE WATCHER IS ITS OWN PROCESS. A long-lived mail connection is a
     background-worker concern; running it inside the web server means it
     restarts on every deployment and duplicates itself across instances.
     Because they are separate processes, notifications travel through the
     database, which is the one thing both already share.

 MEASURED
   7-8 seconds from a message becoming visible in the mailbox to appearing on
   the dashboard. Roughly 4 seconds of that is the ingest itself. Gmail's own
   delivery time - typically another 5-7 seconds from when the sender presses
   send - is separate and outside this system.

 OPERATIONAL NOTE
   Run the watcher alongside the web server. The web server also starts one
   when a dashboard connects, so a single process works in development.

 M17  CLARIFICATION EXCHANGE                                             LIVE
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

 PURPOSE
   Settle a request whose named company matches two insureds equally, by asking
   the person who sent it.

 THE PROBLEM IT SOLVES
   An agency can easily carry "Smart Way Solutions Inc" and "Smart Way
   Solutions LLC" - the same trading name under different entities, with
   different policies and different limits. A request naming "Smartway
   Solutions" fits both exactly as well. Entity resolution correctly refuses.

   Until now that refusal went to a reviewer, who has to guess just as much as
   the machine did: THE AGENCY'S OWN RECORDS DO NOT SAY WHICH OF ITS CLIENTS A
   STRANGER MEANT. The requester does know. They are onboarding one specific
   carrier and have its paperwork in front of them.

 BEHAVIOUR
   Replies in the original thread asking which company is meant, and offers the
   reliable way to say so: a USDOT or MC number. The request waits at "asked
   the requester". When the reply arrives it is recognised as belonging to that
   request, read, and used to resolve it - after which the certificate is
   drafted automatically, exactly as it would have been.

 WHY FEDERAL IDENTIFIERS
   Company names are not unique and never have been. A USDOT number and an MC
   number are issued by the federal government to one motor carrier each.
   Matching then stops being a similarity judgement and becomes a lookup that
   either finds exactly one company or finds none.

   THERE IS NO FUZZY MATCHING ON AN IDENTIFIER, EVER. Digits transposed in a
   DOT number name a completely unrelated carrier, so it matches exactly or not
   at all.

 THIS IS THE ONLY MAIL THE SYSTEM SENDS WITHOUT A HUMAN
   Worth stating plainly, because everything else in this system requires a
   person to press something. The question carries no coverage, no limits, no
   policy numbers and no document - nothing anybody could rely on. It can be
   switched off, in which case those requests go to a reviewer as before.

 GUARDRAILS
   - It asks ONCE. A second unanswered question in one thread reads as a
     malfunction.
   - It names only the companies the requester's own words already implied,
     never the wider client list, and never their addresses or client numbers.
   - It obeys the same recipient rules as certificates, so demonstration data
     cannot generate mail.
   - Our own outgoing mail is stamped and skipped on the way back in. The
     mailbox we read is the mailbox we send from; without this the system would
     answer itself.
   - An unusable reply does NOT trigger a second question. It goes to a
     reviewer, who can read what was actually written.
   - The question we sent is stripped out before the reply is read - it lists
     every candidate and contains example numbers, so reading it back would
     make every reply look ambiguous.

 ASSURANCE   30 automated checks, plus an opt-in live test of the whole
             exchange against a real mailbox.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 M18  VIN DISCLOSURE CONTROL                                             LIVE
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

 PURPOSE
   Decide whether the vehicle schedule carries vehicle identification numbers.

 BEHAVIOUR
   Off unless the request asks for them.

 WHY OFF BY DEFAULT
   A VIN identifies a specific vehicle, and a full schedule with VINs is a
   complete inventory of the insured's rolling stock. Most requests need limits
   and dates, not serial numbers. Printing them anyway discloses more of the
   insured's business than was asked for, to a third party the insured did not
   choose.

   The absence of a request is therefore treated as "do not include", not as
   "no preference".

 DESIGN DECISIONS AND WHY
   - THE INVERSIONS MUST NOT FIRE. "No VINs needed" contains the very word
     being looked for. Declines are checked before requests, and this is
     tested explicitly.

     page split is measured; nothing downstream needs to know.
   - A reviewer can still add them by hand. This decides what the DRAFT
