 PART 1 - LEGAL AND LICENSING
===============================================================================

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 1.1  ACORD FORMS LICENCE                                            BLOCKER
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

 CertFlow reproduces the ACORD 25 Certificate of Liability Insurance and the
 ACORD 101 Additional Remarks Schedule programmatically.

 ACORD forms are copyrighted. Reproducing them in software requires a licence
 from ACORD, and the licence terms govern how the forms may be generated,
 stored and distributed.

 THE AGENCY IS RESPONSIBLE FOR HOLDING THIS LICENCE.

 WHAT TO DO
   - Confirm whether an existing agency licence already covers programmatic
     reproduction, or whether a separate software licence is required.
   - Begin the application early. This is a LONG-LEAD ITEM and is not within
     the supplier's control.
   - Confirm the licensed form editions. The system is calibrated to the
     ACORD 25 (2025/12) and ACORD 101 (2008/01) editions. A different edition
     requires re-derivation of the form coordinate map.

 RISK IF UNRESOLVED
   The system cannot lawfully be used in production. This is the single
   hardest prerequisite to retrofit.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 1.2  AUTHORITY TO ISSUE CERTIFICATES                                BLOCKER
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

 The agency must confirm that the staff who will approve certificates in
 CertFlow hold the appropriate producer licences in the states concerned, and
 that automated preparation with human approval is consistent with the
 agency's errors-and-omissions carrier's expectations.

 CertFlow records who approved each certificate, which supports this. It does
 not verify licensure.

 WHAT TO DO
   - Confirm with the agency's E&O carrier that the workflow is acceptable.
   - Confirm which named individuals may approve.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 1.3  DATA PROTECTION AND PRIVACY                                   REQUIRED
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

 The system holds client business information, policy details, fleet records
 and federal tax identifiers, and it reads a mailbox containing third-party
 correspondence.

 WHAT TO DO
   - Confirm the agency's obligations under the Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act
     Safeguards Rule and any applicable state insurance data security law
     (for example the NAIC Insurance Data Security Model Law as adopted).
   - Execute a data processing agreement between the agency and the supplier.
   - Identify and approve subprocessors: the database host, the application
     host, and the mail provider.
   - Confirm the retention period. The system currently purges stored email
     bodies after 30 days. Confirm this satisfies, and does not conflict with,
     the agency's own record-keeping obligations.

 WHAT THE SYSTEM ALREADY DOES
   - Mail that is not a certificate request is never stored.
   - Stored email bodies carry a purge date set at ingestion.
   - The audit log stores references and hashes, never message content.
   - Per-agency encryption keys allow a complete crypto-shred on request
     without destroying the audit trail.

 PART 2 - DATA
===============================================================================

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 2.1  SOURCE OF POLICY DATA                                          BLOCKER
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

 THIS IS THE LARGEST INTEGRATION DECISION AND IT DRIVES THE TIMELINE.

 Every value CertFlow prints is read from a policy record held in its own
 database. There is currently NO live connection to an agency management
 system. Those records must get in, and stay current.

 THREE ROUTES

   ROUTE A - LIVE API INTEGRATION
     Direct connection to AMS360, Applied Epic or equivalent.
     Requires:   vendor API access (usually licensed separately), API
                 credentials, vendor approval, and documentation.
     Data is:    always current.
     Risk:       vendor may not expose the needed entities, may charge
                 significantly, or may require a partner agreement.

   ROUTE B - SCHEDULED IMPORT
     Recurring export from the management system, imported on a schedule.
     Requires:   an export capability, an agreed file format, a delivery
                 mechanism, and an agreed frequency.
     Data is:    as current as the last export.
     Risk:       a certificate could be issued against a policy that lapsed
                 or changed since the last import. THE EXPORT FREQUENCY MUST
                 BE SHORT ENOUGH THAT THIS CANNOT HAPPEN IN PRACTICE.

   ROUTE C - MAINTAINED IN CERTFLOW
     Policy and fleet data entered and maintained directly.
     Requires:   no integration.
     Data is:    as current as manual maintenance.
     Risk:       creates a second system of record. NOT RECOMMENDED for any
                 agency of meaningful size, because the two will diverge.

 WHAT TO DO
   - Decide the route. This cannot be deferred; it blocks estimation of every
     downstream phase.
   - For Route A: identify the vendor contact and begin the API access
     request.
   - For Route B: provide a sample export.
   - For Route C: confirm who maintains the data and how changes are caught.

 DATA REQUIRED IN ALL CASES
     Insureds        legal name, trading names, address, client number,
                     DOT number, MC number, tax identifier
     Aliases         alternate spellings the agency has seen in the wild.
                     THESE MATERIALLY IMPROVE MATCHING ACCURACY.
     Policies        carrier, NAIC code, policy number, effective and expiry
                     dates, coverage type, limits, additional-insured and
                     waiver-of-subrogation status
     Vehicles        year, make, model, VIN, stated value, deductibles, and
                     the schedule order used by the agency
     Producer        the agency's own name, address, contact, phone and email
                     as they must appear in the PRODUCER box
     Standard text   endorsement and description-of-operations wording, which
                     is printed VERBATIM. The system does not compose legal
                     language.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 2.2  DATA QUALITY                                                  REQUIRED
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

 CONSTRAINT: the system's accuracy is bounded by the accuracy of the records
 it reads. It removes transcription error. It cannot detect that a policy
 record itself is wrong or stale.

 WHAT TO DO
   - Confirm that expired policies are marked as such in the source system.
     CertFlow selects active policies; it relies on that status being correct.
   - Confirm the vehicle schedule is maintained. A missing unit produces a
     certificate that omits it.
   - Supply known aliases. A company that writes in as "Smartway Solutions"
     when the record says "Smart Way Solutions Inc" is matched only because
     the alias exists or the similarity is close enough.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 2.3  HISTORICAL CERTIFICATES                                        ADVISED
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

 CONSTRAINT: certificates issued before go-live do not exist in CertFlow.
 Migration is out of scope by default.

 CONSEQUENCE: a correction to a pre-go-live certificate cannot be issued as a
 revision of it, because the original is not in the system.

 WHAT TO DO
   - Decide whether that is acceptable, or whether a migration is required as
     a change request.


===============================================================================
 PART 3 - EMAIL
===============================================================================

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 3.1  A DEDICATED MAILBOX                                           REQUIRED
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

 CONSTRAINT: the system currently supports Google Workspace / Gmail only.
 Microsoft 365 is not supported in this phase.

 WHAT TO DO
   - Provide a dedicated mailbox used only for certificate requests, for
     example certificates@[agency-domain].
   - Publish that address to brokers, shippers and load boards.
   - Do NOT point CertFlow at a general agency inbox. It will function - the
     classifier filters non-requests - but a dedicated mailbox produces a far
     cleaner audit position and materially fewer misclassifications.

 WHAT THE SYSTEM ALREADY DOES
   - Opens the mailbox READ-ONLY. It cannot mark, move or delete mail.
   - Never stores mail that is not a certificate request.
   - Reports every skipped message and the reason, so an incorrect exclusion
     is reviewable.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 3.2  MAILBOX AUTHORISATION                                          BLOCKER
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

 CURRENT STATE: authentication uses a Google App Password. This is acceptable
 for development against a mailbox the supplier controls. IT IS NOT ACCEPTABLE
 FOR PRODUCTION, because an App Password grants full read and send access to
 the entire account and cannot be scoped or revoked per integration.

 REQUIRED FOR PRODUCTION: OAuth 2.0, scoped to read-only mail access, granted
 by the agency and revocable by the agency without affecting anything else.

 WHAT TO DO
   - Create a Google Cloud project under the agency's own organisation.
   - Configure the consent screen and request the read-only mail scope.
   - Grant administrative consent.
   - Note that Google may require a security assessment for restricted mail
     scopes. THIS IS A LONG-LEAD ITEM. Begin it early.

 SUPPLIER RESPONSIBILITY
   The code change is confined to a single module and is included in scope.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 3.3  OUTBOUND DELIVERY AND SENDER REPUTATION                       REQUIRED
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

 CONSTRAINT: certificates are delivered as replies from the configured
 mailbox. If that mailbox's domain is not properly authenticated, replies
 carrying PDF attachments are liable to be filtered as spam by recipients.

 WHAT TO DO
   - Confirm SPF, DKIM and DMARC are correctly published for the sending
     domain.
   - Confirm the sending address is one that brokers will recognise and trust.
   - Confirm Google Workspace sending limits are adequate for expected volume.

 WHAT THE SYSTEM ALREADY DOES
   - Replies IN THE ORIGINAL THREAD, using the standard threading headers, so
     the certificate lands directly beneath the request.
   - Refuses to send to malformed addresses and to reserved test domains,
     which prevents demonstration data from reaching a real company.
   - Sends only after a human confirms the recipient address.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 3.4  APPROVAL TO WRITE TO REQUESTERS AUTOMATICALLY                 REQUIRED
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

 The system sends ONE kind of message without a person pressing anything: a
 question back to the requester when two of the agency's own clients are named
 too alike to tell apart. It asks which company they meant and offers a USDOT
 or MC number as the reliable way to say so.

 WHY IT EXISTS
   The agency's records genuinely cannot answer the question. "Smart Way
   Solutions Inc" and "Smart Way Solutions LLC" are different companies with
   different policies, and nothing in the file says which one a stranger meant.
   The requester does know. Routing it to a reviewer instead means a person
   guessing on the same information the machine had.

 WHAT IT CONTAINS
   No coverage, no limits, no policy numbers and no document. It names only the
   companies the requester's own words already implied - never the wider client
   list, and never their addresses or client numbers.

 WHAT TO DECIDE
   - Is the agency content for the system to reply to requesters on its own?
   - Does the wording suit the agency's voice? It is a fixed template and can
     be edited.
   - Should it be switched off? It can be, in which case those requests go to
     a reviewer exactly as every other unresolved one does.

 GUARDRAILS ALREADY IN PLACE
   It asks once per request, obeys the same recipient rules as certificates,
   and skips its own output on the way back in.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 3.5  REQUEST FORMAT LIMITS                                          ADVISED
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

 THE SYSTEM CAN INTERPRET
   - Plain text and HTML email.
   - Requests that name the insured in a recognised construction: after
     "COI for", behind an "Insured:" label, in the subject line, and similar.
   - English.

 THE SYSTEM CANNOT INTERPRET
   - Requests where the insured is named ONLY inside a PDF or image
     attachment. There is no document text recognition.
   - Requests that name the insured only by implication ("the usual
     certificate", "same as last time").
   - Languages other than English.
   - Requests submitted through a web portal rather than email.

 WHAT HAPPENS INSTEAD
   These are not lost. They arrive on the dashboard marked as needing a human,
   with the email shown and a searchable list of insureds. A reviewer resolves
   them in a few seconds. THIS IS THE DESIGNED BEHAVIOUR, NOT A FAILURE.

 WHAT TO DO
   - Supply [N] real, anonymised requests during onboarding so the extraction
     rules can be calibrated against the phrasing the agency actually
     receives.
   - Confirm whether requesters routinely supply USDOT or MC numbers. Where
     they do, ambiguous requests resolve themselves without a reviewer.


===============================================================================
 PART 4 - IDENTITY AND ACCESS
===============================================================================

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 4.1  IDENTITY PROVIDER                                              BLOCKER
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

 CONSTRAINT: CertFlow stores no passwords, by design. There is no password
 column in the database and no password reset flow. Authentication is
 delegated entirely to the agency's identity provider.

 SUPPORTED: Google Workspace, or Microsoft Entra ID, via OpenID Connect.

 WHAT TO DO
   - Register CertFlow as an application in the agency's identity tenant.
   - Provide the issuer URL, client identifier and client secret.
   - Configure the redirect URI supplied by the supplier.
   - CONFIRM MULTI-FACTOR AUTHENTICATION IS ENFORCED. The system relies on the
     identity provider for this; it does not implement its own.

 CONSEQUENCE IF NOT DONE
   Sign-in falls back to a development path that REFUSES TO OPERATE in a
   production environment. A missing configuration therefore locks everyone
   out rather than letting anyone in - which is the intended failure mode.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 4.2  USER PROVISIONING                                             REQUIRED
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

 CONSTRAINT: users are NEVER created automatically at sign-in. A record must
 already exist, or anyone with a Google account could reach the agency's data.
 There is currently no self-service user administration screen; users are
 provisioned by the supplier or by direct database operation.

 WHAT TO DO
   - Supply the list of users, their email addresses, and their roles:

       ADMINISTRATOR   full access, including configuration
       REVIEWER        may draft, edit, approve and send
       READ-ONLY       may view, may not approve or send

   - Nominate who may APPROVE and SEND. This is the accountable act; the
     system records the individual against every issued certificate.
   - Define the process for removing access when someone leaves. Suspension
     takes effect on the user's NEXT REQUEST, not at their next sign-in.


===============================================================================
 PART 5 - INFRASTRUCTURE
===============================================================================

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 5.1  HOSTING AND DATABASE                                          REQUIRED
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

 REQUIRED
   - A PostgreSQL 15 or later instance, not publicly reachable.
   - A Node.js 20 or later application host.
   - TLS on all endpoints.
   - Automated backups with a confirmed and TESTED restore procedure.
   - Secret storage for the master encryption key, session signing secret,
     database credentials and mail credentials.

 DECISIONS THE AGENCY MUST MAKE
   - Data residency. Where must the data physically reside?
   - Retention of backups, and whether backups fall inside the deletion
     obligations in 1.3.
   - Who holds the master encryption key, and what the recovery procedure is
     if it is lost. LOSING IT RENDERS ENCRYPTED DATA PERMANENTLY UNREADABLE.
     That property is deliberate - it is what makes crypto-shredding work -
     but it means key custody is a serious operational responsibility.

 KNOWN CONSTRAINT
   The current database provider's direct endpoint is IPv6-only. A deployment
   without IPv6 connectivity must use the provider's session pooler endpoint.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 5.2  AVAILABILITY EXPECTATIONS                                      ADVISED
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

 CONSTRAINT: the system now runs as TWO PROCESSES - a web server and a mailbox
 watcher. The watcher must be kept running for mail to be ingested when nobody
 has the dashboard open.

 WHAT TO DECIDE
   - How the watcher process is supervised and restarted. It reconnects on its
     own after a dropped connection, but not after the host restarts.
   - Who is alerted if it stops. A stopped watcher looks exactly like a quiet
     mailbox from the dashboard's point of view.

 EXPECTED PERFORMANCE
   7-8 seconds from a request landing in the mailbox to appearing on screen,
   with no user interaction and no page reload. The sender's own mail provider
   typically adds another 5-7 seconds before the message is visible to us at
   all, which is outside this system.

 THE DATABASE CONNECTION MODE MATTERS
   The two processes notify each other through PostgreSQL. This requires a
   SESSION-mode connection. A transaction-pooled connection will not deliver
   the notifications, and the dashboard would fall back to a slow refresh
   without any error being raised.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 5.3  MONITORING                                                    REQUIRED
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

 WHAT TO DO
   - Decide who is alerted if the mailbox connection fails.
   - Decide who is alerted if the application is unavailable.
   - Establish an escalation path.

 WHAT THE SYSTEM ALREADY DOES
   Repeated mailbox failures, or any permission error, STOP the automatic
   check and display that on screen with a retry control. It does not fail
   silently. But nothing currently notifies anyone who is not looking at the
   screen.


===============================================================================
 PART 6 - BUSINESS PROCESS
===============================================================================

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 6.1  THE REVIEWER ROLE                                             REQUIRED
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

 CONSTRAINT AND DESIGN INTENT: the system prepares documents. IT DOES NOT
 ISSUE OR SEND THEM. A person must approve and a person must send.

 This is not a limitation to be engineered away. It is the control that keeps
 professional accountability with a licensed human being, and it is what makes
 the audit trail meaningful.

 WHAT TO DECIDE
   - Who reviews, and during which hours.
   - Target turnaround from request received to certificate sent.
   - What happens during absence, leave, and peak periods.
   - What a reviewer should do when the certificate looks wrong. (The
     underlying policy record is corrected in the source system, and the
     certificate is regenerated.)

 IMPLICATION FOR EXPECTED BENEFIT
   The time saved is transcription time, not review time. If the agency
   expects certificates to leave without human involvement, that expectation
   is inconsistent with the system as designed and as scoped.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 6.2  HANDLING ABSTENTIONS                                          REQUIRED
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

 The system deliberately declines to identify an insured when two similarly
 named companies are close, or when the email never said clearly.

 EXPECT A PROPORTION OF REQUESTS TO REQUIRE HUMAN IDENTIFICATION. The exact
 proportion depends on the agency's client list and its correspondents'
 phrasing, and can only be established against real mail.

 WHAT TO DECIDE
   - Who handles these, and how quickly.
   - Note that supplying good aliases (2.1) directly reduces the count.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 6.3  VOLUME                                                         ADVISED
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

 WHAT TO PROVIDE
   - Expected certificate requests per day, and peak.
   - Typical fleet size. Fleets larger than roughly six units overflow to a
     second page, which is handled automatically but should be expected.
   - Number of active insureds. Matching accuracy is affected by how many
     similarly named companies exist in the list.

===============================================================================
 SUMMARY - THE FIVE THINGS THAT WILL DELAY GO-LIVE
===============================================================================

 In order of likelihood, based on what depends on third parties:

  1.  THE ACORD FORMS LICENCE. Outside everyone's control, long lead time,
      and cannot be worked around.

  2.  THE POLICY DATA DECISION. Blocks estimation of every downstream phase.
      Route A depends on a vendor who may say no, or may charge heavily.

  3.  GOOGLE'S MAIL SCOPE REVIEW. Restricted scopes may trigger a security
      assessment.

  4.  DATA QUALITY IN THE SOURCE SYSTEM. Aliases and vehicle schedules are
      frequently incomplete, and this is usually discovered during acceptance
      testing rather than before it.

  5.  REVIEWER AVAILABILITY. The system produces documents that require a
      person. If that person is not assigned, the throughput benefit does not
      materialise regardless of how well the software works.
