 1. PURPOSE
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

This document defines the work to be performed in delivering CertFlow, a
system that automates the issuance of ACORD 25 Certificates of Liability
Insurance for a trucking-focused insurance agency.

It states what is included, what is explicitly excluded, what the client must
provide, and how completion is judged.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 2. BACKGROUND AND BUSINESS PROBLEM
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

An agency writing commercial trucking receives a continuous stream of requests
for certificates of insurance. They arrive by email from brokers, shippers,
load boards and lenders. Each one requires a person to:

   (a) read the email and work out which insured it concerns;
   (b) look that insured up in the agency management system;
   (c) transcribe policy numbers, limits, dates and the vehicle schedule onto
       an ACORD 25 form;
   (d) overflow the vehicle list onto an ACORD 101 when it does not fit;
   (e) email the finished certificate back to whoever asked.

Steps (a) through (d) are mechanical and are where transcription errors enter.
A certificate that misstates coverage is a professional liability exposure for
the agency, not merely an inconvenience.

CertFlow automates (a) through (d) and leaves (e) as a deliberate human act.

Where step (a) cannot be settled - because two of the agency's own clients are
named too alike to tell apart - the system asks the requester directly rather
than putting an unanswerable question in front of a reviewer. See 5.12.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 3. BUSINESS OBJECTIVE
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

 3.1  Reduce the handling of a routine certificate request from minutes of
      manual transcription to a review of a finished document.

 3.2  Eliminate transcription as a source of error. Every value printed on a
      certificate is read from a policy record; none is typed by an operator
      at issuance time.

 3.3  Preserve human accountability. No certificate is issued or sent without
      a named person approving it, and that approval is recorded immutably.

 3.4  Produce a defensible record. For any certificate ever issued, the agency
      can show what was sent, to whom, when, by whom, and what the underlying
      policy data said on that date.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 4. GUIDING PRINCIPLE
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The system is built on one rule, and it governs every design decision in it:

      THE EMAIL DECIDES WHAT WAS ASKED FOR.
      THE DATABASE DECIDES WHAT IS TRUE.

Interpretation of an inbound email may only ever select WHICH insured a
request concerns. It has no mechanism to supply a policy number, a limit, a
date or a coverage. Those are read from the agency's own records.

The consequence is that the worst outcome of a misread email is that a
reviewer is shown the wrong company and rejects it. The system is
structurally incapable of producing a certificate that asserts coverage which
does not exist.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 5. SCOPE - DELIVERED AND OPERATIONAL
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The following is built, tested and demonstrable today.

 5.1  MAIL INGESTION
      Reads a designated mailbox over IMAP. The mailbox is opened read-only,
      so the system cannot mark, move or delete the agency's mail. Repeated
      polling is safe: de-duplication is enforced by a database constraint,
      not by flagging messages as read.

 5.2  REQUEST FILTERING
      Classifies each message before it is stored. Mail that is not a
      certificate request is never written to the database at all. Every
      skipped message and the reason for skipping it is reported on screen,
      so a wrong exclusion is reviewable rather than silent.

 5.3  INSURED IDENTIFICATION
      Extracts candidate company names from the request and resolves them
      against the client list using fuzzy matching over legal names and known
      aliases. Abstains by design: a near-tie between two similarly named
      companies is routed to a human rather than decided by a narrow margin.

 5.4  CERTIFICATE HOLDER IDENTIFICATION
      Reads the holder from the requester's email signature. This is the only
      certificate field sourced from the email rather than the database, and
      necessarily so - the holder is whoever wrote in, and the agency has no
      record of them until they do.

 5.5  AUTOMATIC DRAFTING
      As soon as a request resolves to an insured, the certificate is
      assembled from that insured's policy, vehicle and party records. A
      reviewer opens a finished document to check, not an empty one to
      trigger.

 5.6  DOCUMENT GENERATION
      Produces a pixel-accurate ACORD 25 (2025/12 edition), with automatic
      overflow to an ACORD 101 Additional Remarks Schedule when the vehicle
      schedule exceeds page one. Line breaking is width-aware, so the
      on-screen form and the generated PDF break in identical places.

 5.7  REVIEW AND EDIT
      An on-screen form overlaid on the actual ACORD artwork. Every field is
      editable while the certificate is a draft.

 5.8  APPROVAL AND ISSUANCE
      A named user approves; the document becomes issued and its content is
      frozen. Issued certificates are never re-derived from live policy data,
      because policies renew and a reprint must show what was certified on the
      day. Corrections create a new revision of the same certificate number,
      never a new number.

 5.9  DELIVERY
      Replies in the original email thread with the PDF attached, so the
      certificate lands directly beneath the request that asked for it.
      Delivery is a separate, explicit human action.

      NO CERTIFICATE IS EVER SENT AUTOMATICALLY. The system sends exactly one
      kind of message on its own - the clarification question at 5.12 - and
      that carries no coverage, no limits and no document.

 5.10 CONTINUOUS MONITORING, PUSHED TO THE SCREEN
      A watcher process holds one connection to the mailbox open. The mail
      server announces new mail on it, and the same connection is re-checked
      every couple of seconds as a guarantee. Changes are pushed to the browser;
      nothing is polled by the reviewer's machine and no page reloads.
      Measured at 7-8 seconds from a message landing in the mailbox to appearing
      on screen, with no user interaction. There is no "check for new email"
      button because there is nothing for it to do.

 5.11 UNREAD COUNT
      The dashboard shows how many requests nobody has opened yet. Recorded
      against the request rather than the browser, so it is the same figure on
      every machine.

 5.12 THE SYSTEM ASKS WHEN TWO CLIENTS ARE TOO ALIKE
      Where a request names something that fits two insureds equally - "Smart
      Way Solutions Inc" and "Smart Way Solutions LLC" - the system replies to
      the requester asking which they mean, and offers the reliable way to say
      so: a USDOT or MC number. Federal identifiers belong to exactly one motor
      carrier, so the answer resolves the request precisely rather than by
      similarity. The request waits at "asked the requester" until the reply
      arrives, then completes on its own.

      This is the ONLY message the system sends without a person pressing
      something. It carries no coverage, no limits and no document. It can be
      switched off, in which case those requests go to a reviewer instead.

 5.13 VEHICLE IDENTIFICATION NUMBERS ARE WITHHELD UNLESS ASKED FOR
      A fleet schedule with VINs is an inventory of the insured's vehicles.
      Most requests need limits and dates, not serial numbers, so they are
      omitted unless the request asks. A reviewer can still add them by hand.

 5.14 AUTHENTICATION AND TENANT ISOLATION
      Sign-in through the agency's own identity provider (OIDC). No passwords
      are stored anywhere in the system. Data separation is enforced by
      database row-level security, not by application-side filtering.

 5.15 AUDIT TRAIL
      Every state change is recorded in an append-only, hash-chained log.
      Update and delete are revoked at the database level, so immutability is
      a grant rather than a promise.

 5.16 VERIFICATION HARNESS
      236 automated checks across ten suites, including 45 executed in a real
      browser, plus three opt-in tests that send genuine email and prove the
      whole chain against a real mailbox.

 6. SCOPE - REMAINING WORK
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The following is required before production use and is included in this
engagement.

 6.1  POLICY DATA INTEGRATION                         [MAJOR - SEE SECTION 7]
      CertFlow currently reads policy, vehicle and client data from its own
      database. There is no live connection to an agency management system.
      Populating and keeping that data current is the single largest piece of
      remaining work.

 6.2  PER-TENANT MAILBOX AUTHORISATION
      Replace the current App Password mechanism with OAuth 2.0 scoped to
      read-only mail access, so the agency can grant and revoke access without
      sharing account credentials.

 6.3  RATE LIMITING
      Request throttling on all API routes.

 6.4  SCHEDULED DATA PURGE
      A background job enforcing the retention clock already recorded against
      every stored email. The mechanism exists and is currently run manually.

 6.5  AUDIT LOG VIEWER
      A user interface over the audit trail. The data is complete and
      queryable today; it has no screen.

 6.6  FIELD-LEVEL ENCRYPTION ACTIVATION
      Envelope encryption with per-tenant keys is implemented and tested but
      is not yet wired to the columns it is intended to protect.

 6.7  PRODUCTION DEPLOYMENT
      Environment provisioning, secret management, backup verification,
      monitoring and alerting.

 6.8  USER ACCEPTANCE TESTING AND HANDOVER
      Including operator documentation and training.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 7. CRITICAL DEPENDENCY - SOURCE OF POLICY DATA
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

This section is called out separately because it determines the effort,
timeline and viability of the whole engagement, and it cannot be resolved by
the supplier alone.

Every value CertFlow prints is read from a policy record. Those records must
get into the system. There are three possible routes:

   ROUTE A - LIVE INTEGRATION
      A direct connection to the agency management system (AMS360, Applied
      Epic or equivalent). Requires vendor API access, which is typically
      licensed separately and may require the vendor's approval. Effort
      depends entirely on what that vendor exposes.

   ROUTE B - SCHEDULED IMPORT
      A recurring export from the management system, imported on a schedule.
      Lower integration risk. Data is only as fresh as the export cadence,
      which must be short enough that a certificate cannot be issued against
      a lapsed policy.

   ROUTE C - MANAGED WITHIN CERTFLOW
      Policy and fleet data maintained directly in CertFlow. No integration
      required, but this creates a second system of record and is not
      recommended for an agency of meaningful size.

 ACTION REQUIRED: The client must confirm which route applies, and provide the
 relevant vendor contacts or export samples, before this element can be
 estimated or scheduled.

 Until this is resolved, all other work proceeds against representative data.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 8. OUT OF SCOPE
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The following are explicitly excluded unless separately agreed in writing:

  -  ACORD forms other than the 25 and 101.
  -  Certificate issuance for lines of business other than commercial
     trucking.
  -  Policy administration, rating, quoting, binding or endorsement.
  -  Claims handling of any kind.
  -  Accounting, commission tracking or invoicing.
  -  Migration of historical certificates issued before go-live.
  -  Native mobile applications. The interface is responsive but
     browser-based.
  -  Mail providers other than Google Workspace / Gmail in this phase.
  -  Procurement of the ACORD forms licence (see Section 9.1).
  -  Third-party licence fees, hosting costs and vendor API fees.
  -  Legal or regulatory review of the agency's compliance obligations.

 9. CLIENT RESPONSIBILITIES
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

 9.1  ACORD FORMS LICENCE
      ACORD forms are copyrighted. The client is responsible for holding a
      valid licence permitting programmatic reproduction of the ACORD 25 and
      ACORD 101. This is a precondition of production use and is a long-lead
      item.

 9.2  A DEDICATED MAILBOX
      A Google Workspace mailbox used solely for certificate requests, with
      administrative consent to authorise read access.

 9.3  IDENTITY PROVIDER ACCESS
      Registration of CertFlow as an application in the client's Google
      Workspace or Microsoft Entra tenant.

 9.4  POLICY DATA ACCESS
      Per Section 7.

 9.5  A NAMED BUSINESS OWNER
      A single point of contact empowered to make decisions on form content,
      standard endorsement wording, and acceptance.

 9.6  REVIEWER AVAILABILITY
      The system requires a human to approve and send. The client must
      identify who performs this role and during which hours.

 9.7  TIMELY REVIEW
      Feedback on deliverables within [N] working days of submission.

 10. ASSUMPTIONS
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

 10.1  Requests arrive predominantly in English, as text or HTML email.
       Certificate requests contained only inside scanned attachments are not
       interpreted.

 10.2  The ACORD 25 (2025/12) and ACORD 101 (2008/01) editions remain current
       for the term of this engagement. A new edition requires re-derivation
       of the coordinate map and is a change request.

 10.3  Certificate volumes remain within [N] requests per day.

 10.4  A single agency tenant is in scope for this phase. The architecture is
       multi-tenant, but only one tenant is being commissioned.

 10.5  Standard endorsement and description-of-operations wording is supplied
       by the client and printed verbatim. The system does not generate legal
       language.

 12. ACCEPTANCE CRITERIA
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The engagement is complete when all of the following are demonstrated on the
client's own data, in the client's own environment:

 A1  A genuine certificate request emailed to the production mailbox appears
     on the dashboard without manual intervention.

 A2  The insured is correctly identified for [N] out of [N] representative
     requests supplied by the client.

 A3  Requests the system cannot confidently identify are routed to a human
     rather than guessed.

 A4  A generated ACORD 25 matches a certificate produced manually by the
     client's own staff, field for field, for [N] agreed test cases.

 A5  Vehicle schedules exceeding page one overflow correctly to an ACORD 101.

 A6  An approved certificate is delivered as a threaded reply with the PDF
     attached, to an address the reviewer confirmed.

 A7  Every action above appears in the audit log with actor, timestamp and
     subject.

 A8  A user whose access is withdrawn is denied on their next request.

 A9  The full automated verification suite passes in the client environment.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 15. CHANGE CONTROL
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Any variation to this scope is raised as a written change request stating the
change, its effect on cost, and its effect on schedule. Work on a change
begins only after both parties sign it.

The following are treated as changes, not defects:

  -  A new or revised ACORD form edition.
  -  Additional ACORD forms.
  -  A change of agency management system or data source after P0.
  -  Additional tenants or agencies.
  -  Mail providers beyond Google Workspace.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 16. RISKS
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

 R1  Policy data source unresolved (Section 7). Highest risk on the
     engagement. Mitigation: resolve at P0 before committing downstream dates.

 R2  Agency management system vendor does not expose a usable API, or charges
     prohibitively for it. Mitigation: Route B (scheduled import) as fallback.

 R3  ACORD licence not in place at go-live. Mitigation: client to begin the
     application immediately.

 R4  Request phrasing in the client's real mail differs materially from the
     patterns tested. Mitigation: client to supply [N] real anonymised
     requests during P0 for calibration.

 R5  Reviewer capacity. The system removes transcription, not judgement. If
     nobody approves certificates, nothing is sent.
