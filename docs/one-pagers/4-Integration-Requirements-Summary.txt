 Most of what stands between a working demonstration and a working business
 system is not software. It is licences, data access, permissions, and
 agreement about who does what.

 START THESE TODAY - THEY DEPEND ON OTHER PEOPLE

  1. LICENCE FOR THE INDUSTRY FORMS
     The certificate forms are copyrighted. The agency needs a licence
     allowing software to produce them. Slow, outside our control, and it
     cannot be worked around. Nothing goes live without it.

  2. PERMISSION TO READ THE AGENCY MAILBOX
     Needs a project set up under the agency's own Google account, with a
     consent step. Google may run a security review of this. Also slow.

  3. WHERE POLICY DATA COMES FROM        <-- BLOCKS EVERYTHING ELSE
     The system prints policy details from a database that must be filled
     from the agency's real system. Three options:

       A. A live connection to their main system. Always current, but
          depends on that supplier offering access - which usually costs
          extra and sometimes is not available at all.
       B. A regular file export, loaded on a schedule. Simpler and safer to
          build. Must run often enough that a certificate can never be
          issued against a policy that has since lapsed.
       C. Kept up to date by hand inside our system. No connection needed,
          but creates a second set of records that will drift out of step.
          Not recommended.

     Until the client picks one, no later stage can be estimated or dated.

 NEEDED BEFORE GO-LIVE
  4. A dedicated email address for requests, published to brokers - not the
     general agency inbox.
  5. Registration with the agency's staff sign-in, with multi-factor sign-in
     switched on. Without it nobody can log in at all.
  6. The list of staff and what each may do: view only, prepare and send, or
     full access.
  7. The data itself: client companies, their known alternative spellings,
     policies, vehicle lists, the agency's own contact block, and any
     standard wording that must appear on certificates.
  8. Confirmation from the agency's insurer that this way of working is
     acceptable to them.
  9. A data protection agreement, and confirmation that deleting stored
     emails after 30 days fits the agency's own record-keeping rules.
 10. Somewhere to run it: a private database, a server, backups that have
     actually been tested, and safe storage for the system's keys.
 11. Confirmation the agency's email domain is set up properly, so
     certificates do not land in brokers' spam folders.
 12. Named people who will check and send certificates, and their hours.

 13. APPROVAL FOR THE SYSTEM TO EMAIL REQUESTERS ON ITS OWN
     When two of the agency's clients are named too alike, the system replies
     to the requester asking which one, and for a USDOT or MC number. It is
     the only message it sends without a person. No coverage, no document -
     just a question. The agency needs to approve the wording, or say to
     switch it off.

 14. SOMEONE TO SUPERVISE THE MAILBOX WATCHER
     The system runs as two programs. If the one watching the mailbox stops,
     the dashboard looks exactly like a quiet mailbox. Someone must be alerted.

 WHAT THE SYSTEM CANNOT DO - SET EXPECTATIONS ON THESE
   - It cannot read a request that names the company only inside an attached
     PDF or scanned image. These still reach the screen for a person; they
     are just not automatic.
   - English only. Google email only - Microsoft mailboxes not yet supported.
   - It cannot tell that a policy record is itself wrong or out of date. It
     removes typing errors, not bad source data.
   - It leaves vehicle ID numbers off unless the request asks for them.

 THE FIVE THINGS MOST LIKELY TO DELAY GO-LIVE
   1. The forms licence                    4. Gaps in the agency's own data
   2. The policy data decision             5. Nobody assigned to check and
   3. Google's review of mailbox access       send certificates
